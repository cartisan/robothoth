## Misc
- Rule of thumb: 150 PSI = 1 MPa
- Rule of thumb: 1t of cement releases 800kg of CO2
- The linear relationship between fineness and higher strength breaks down after a certain threshold, from which on grinding the cement fine does not linearly increase strength any more

## Chemical reaction
- Clinker is most important for strength
- TiO2 (Titan Dioxid) is important for strength, only present in slag based cements and correlates with slag content
- Slag is important for 28d strength but not that much for early strength
- SO3 (Sulfur Trioxide) increases hardening speed, but decreases overall cs
- C2S has less influence on early strength and more and 28d strength
- SI02 only correlates with strength when puzzolans are present <-- is the S in C2S/C3S
- CaO <-- is the C in C2S/C3S
- Chlorine is good for early strength
- limestone increases water demand
- Na2O equivalent = Na2O + 0.658 * K2O
	- also called alkalis
	- A low Na2O is a sign of good clinker quality
		- Na2O content decreases in summer, because sodium is introduced via road salt in winter via the fuels (fluff)
	- K2O comes from quarry

- Clinker phases: 
	- Measured by XRD, come from reaction in oven
	- alite (C3S), belite (C2S), aluminat (C3A), ferruminat (C4AF) which are minerals, i.e. cristalline  structures
		- react with water (hydration), water is bound in the mineral structure
		- different phases have different hydration speed
		- other components (like slag or fly ash) react differently to water
			- gives us Cem IIs, while Cem Is only have the clinker for hydration
	- Hydration products (of alite and belite): 
		- calcium silicate: CSH -- these are amorphous, we can't see them in XRD
		- portlandite: Ca(OH)2 -- measures in XRD
	- belite increases early strength, while alite increases final strength 

- Sulfate carriers (CaSO4) $\cdot$ X H20:
	- gypsum (CaSO4 $\cdot$ H20), anhydrite (CaSO4 $\cdot$ 0.5 H20, "Halbhydrat"), bassanite (CaSO4 $\cdot$ 0 H20, "Hemihydrat")
	- help steer out C3A
	- Calcium sources with different water rations
	- Are added after oven, so they do not burn
	- First to react
		- reacting with aluminat to form ettringite (very temperature dependent, so might disappear from XRD fast)
	- Make reaction slower, since it binds water away
	- Affect setting time

- [clinker-slag-calcinated clay cements](https://www.vdz-online.de/wissensportal/publikationen/forschungsergebnisse-leistungsfaehigkeit-ternaerer-zemente-mit-portlandzementklinker-huettensand-und-calciniertem-ton-als-hauptbestandteil)
	- Calcinated clay reactivity has no influence on early strength, weak influence on 28d strength, but by 91d, clay influence is as important as clinker influence. The reason is that the puzzolanic reaction is very slow. This can make it hard to create R class cements when too much clinker is substituted just by clay.
	- 25-55% CO2 emissions can be saved by using the cements they tested in comparison to a Cem I. The influence of slag and clays on CO2 emissions is comparable. However, clay is more easily grindable than slag or clinker

### XRD
- expect 2% measurement error
- only detects cristalline structures that are large enough for xray reflection
	- finer grinding gives better precision, but of you get to nanometers, everything gets amorph and not measurable
- best for comparison of relative peak heights, not absolute peak highs (whcih might vary due to sample prep, machine age, ...)
	- XRD devices excel in comparing successive samples, not in giving correct absolute values (that requires complex methods in a uni setting)
- see also: [[XRD Device Q&A]]

## Mill
- Wattage: Power consumption of mill stays the same, no matter what cement we put in or how coarsely we grind
	- the only thing that changes with higher fineness is that we get less output per time (because cement has to go multiple rounds through the mills)
- Input: only measuring what goes into the mill in terms of all ingredients (inflow)
- Throughput: kWh/t --> Wattage / Input
- That's how we measure "electricity costs" for grinding
### Seperate Grinding
- CEM X is **exclusively produced using separate grinding**
	- clinker and slag need to be ground very fine to be super reactive (d' = 8-10 micro meters)
	- limestone should be ground rather coarse so it doesn't increase water demand too much
