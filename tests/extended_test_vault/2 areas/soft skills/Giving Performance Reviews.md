## Tenets
- Bad perf reviews are very likely to break engineer-manager trust
- Perf reviews of substance need time, which managers need to actively invest
- Acknowledgement of achievements is as important as ratings/numbers
## Bad performance reviews
- Zero specifics: 
	- no examples for points made
	- no constructive feedback
	- means manager doesn't really know you/your work
- Manager/engineer disconnect
	- manager only mentions side line works, but doesn't acknowledge impactful contributions
	- manager probably doesn't know what you work on
- Only numbers
	- just gives a pay rise and bonus, but not feedback on performance
- One sided review
	- negative feedback backed up with examples
	- examples are one-sided or miss key details
	- no open discussion of examples
- Biased review
	- recency or gender bias
- Surprising review
	- good reviews should bring lots of new information, but very little surprises
	- if surprises are involved than continuous feedback is missing in company
## Good performance reviews
- Levels and expectations need to be well defined at beginning --> progression framework
- Ambiguity in assessments leads to bias --> find ways to use rubrics and prompts to be consistent and fair
- Review should have no surprises --> feedback should happen continously
- Goals:
	- **provide fair, unbiased and clear feedback**: what were expectations, and were they met?
	- **build trust**: open, fair and two-sided conversation where both sides feel heard
	- **motivate**: acknowledge all that was good and outline what to focus on next

## Preparing for Reviews
- At start of report:
	- Understand their goals and motivations
	- Clarify manager role: give regular feedback, mentor/coach, provide opportunity for growth
	- Agree on regular 1:1
- Weeks before review
	- Collect What and How
		- 1:1 Notes
		- Projects engineer contributed to
		- Output: Code, Documentation, customer interactions
		- Feedback: Self-review, feedback given (MRs or personal), peer feedback
		--> Goal is to uncover all great work a report did, even the things they forgot
	- If report has [[Performance Self Review]] or [[Work Log]] ask them to provide those
- Writing
	- List out work done, on delivery check if anything is missing --> creates trust by showing that manager sees work and wants to give a fair review
	- List competencies and expectations for meeting them --> set up bar to pass
	- Use first and second list to assess if competencies are met, exceeded or not
	- Provide examples and specific feedback for improvement
		- especially important when someone did not meet expectations
		- feedback needs to be specific and actionable
	- Short summary: where is person against current level and how close to promotion
	- Suggest top 3 focus areas
		- Should be specific and motivating, so person feels like they know how to improve
	- Review whole thing and make sure every step fits overall message at the end, and if not reflect why
- Calibration
	- compare perf reviews side by side
	- focus on high and low performers
	- also focus on people that are different from manager, to ensure fairness to them

## Delivering the Review
- Separate perf feedback and compensation
	- once people here money numbers, their mind goes away from hearing feedback
- Set expectations: format, access to written feedback, exchange after each session
- List achievements, ask if something is missing
- Provide feedback on each area
	- Avoid feedback sandwich and send one message
	- Encourage people to share their thoughts after each area
	- Pay attention to body language, if negative prod on their thoughts
- In the end, ask to reflect on review
- If people disagree:
	- Be open to possible of lacking context or misinterpreting
	- If something was awry, ask to provide details and examples --> schedule follow up meeting with revised rating
	- it is important to know if people are unhappy with their qualitative assessment --> needed to assess their level of trust
