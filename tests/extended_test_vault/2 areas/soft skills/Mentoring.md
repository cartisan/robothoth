- A a learning relationship between an experienced person and someone who wants to grow
- Can have a limited temporal scope (e.g. onboarding), but also run indefinite
- Follows a regular cadence of meetings

## Kick off
- Setup dedicated meeting to have time for the discussion
- Mentee comes prepared:
	- **Background**. Get to know both parties
	- **Goals**. What are you hoping to gain from this relationship? Where would you like to grow? -- actually interesting for both parties
	- **Expectations**. What are the expectations both parties have towards each other?
	- **Topics**. What areas are you interested in? Where would you like to grow? Is there something more pressing to discuss?
	- **Cadence**. How often do the parties meet and for how long? Are random pings between meetings OK?
	- **Short term focus**. Where would mentee like to grow in during the next months?
	- **Evaluating**. progress. What criteria would help to assess how efficient this mentorship is?
	- **Challenges**. It's nice to lay out things that might make this relationship hard.

## Mentee
- As a mentee, you need to invest time and effort in mentorship, to get value out of it
- Come prepared to the catch-ups with your mentor
- Bring challenges, wins, and areas you'd like to get their input on
- Have a list of top things you'd like to talk through with them
- Consider sharing a running doc with mentor, updating between meetings:
	- **Key things that happened since last time**. Fill in mentor briefly on key things that happened since last time. Keep this short.
	- **Reflecting on action items / guidance / discussions from last time**. When doing something talked about last time, like trying out an approach or reading a book, share the results. This is useful for both, mentor gets to learn something interesting.
	- **A current challenge**, talking through it and brainstorming on an approach to solve it.
	- **A recent success**. Describe a situation handled well and why it went well, asking mentor to share their thoughts.
## Mentor
- Clarify how much time you can commit to
- Tell your mentee what you're expecting from them, e.g.
	- Should they bring a list of topics to each meeting
	- What about pings between meetings
### Advice
- Most important part is listening, not solving mentees problems
	- Talk about whats going on and how they feel about this
	- Try starting the conversation with questions that help you listen, like 
		- _"What's on your mind?"_
		- "_What are you struggling with?"_ 
		- "_What are you working on?"_ 
		- "_What are your goals for the week?"_
	- Delay sharing solutions for as long as possible
		- You want thementee to get to solve their problems themselves, with as little direct guidance from you as possible
		- Ask questions, offer alternatives, and hold back telling people what to do
		- Help mentees come up with multiple solutions to their problems and help them articulate the tradeoffs themselves
- Discuss specific situations
	- Brought in by mentee or noticed by mentor
	- Topics can be from communication, cultural issues, deep technical questions, code reviews
	- Provide context and perspective, have you handled a situation like this before? What were you struggling with in such situations? How do things work in the company with stuff like that?
- Leverage your network
	- Especially powerful when you work at the same company, put them in touch with other people who can help
	- Making introductions and asking others to spend some time with the mentee goes a long way
- Be supportive
	- let them know that you are on their side
	- People who approach you for mentorship are not only looking for advice but also support
