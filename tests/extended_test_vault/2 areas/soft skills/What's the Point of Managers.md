## Main Argument
As automated agents take over and speed up the execution of daily tasks, the traditional role of a manager as a supervisor of work is becoming obsolete. The "point" of a manager is no longer to manage the *work itself*, but to manage the **judgment** and **development** of the people doing it.
## Main Tasks of a Modern Manager
### Own the outcomes
* When producing becomes cheap, what differentiates good from bad teams are outcomes
	* Did it move the needle? Did it solve the right problem? Was this the highest leverage usage of our time?
	* Take ownership but also responsibility for final result.
	* **The more agents are doing the execution, the more important it is that someone is accountable for whether the execution added up to anything.**
### Create the shared brain
- Managers decide which context and additional information needs distribution to the team, and how to do so efficiently
- In agent times, this turns into also **maintaining actual context files that are used by team members when interacting with their agents**
	- Whats the source of truth for whole team?
	- How do we integrate competing ways of encoding context?
	- How does context become outdated?
### Build team's judgement
- Monitor: do I have the right people, and are they getting better?
	- Enable training, pairing, mentoring, and giving stretch assignments to force growth
- In agent times: **make sure the people get better, even when agents take over a lot of the actual doing**
	- People get fewer reps in, how do they build better judgement?
	- Higher velocity doesn't mean better understanding, now
		- Slow team down sometimes to ensure they learn; the team itself will always optimize for shipping not learning
### Decide what not to work on
- It's really easy now to run 10x faster in the wrong direction
- **Be opinionated about where attention goes**
	- What are we working on and why? What are we saying no to?
	- Where does the team need to slow down and think?
### Be the connective tissue
- Make sure your team’s work fits the bigger picture
- Negotiating for resources, making the case for your team’s priorities
- Translating what leadership wants into something your team can actually act on
- Ensure people above you understand what’s happening, what’s working, what’s stuck, what they should be worried about.
- Overall: **read the room across boundaries and building relationships**
