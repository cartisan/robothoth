- Friction is the accumulation of small, unexpected problems when executing a (simple) plan
- Friction leads to delays in plan execution and stress
- Friction can not be avoided, but systems and processes can be designed to be as robust as possible to friction

## Properties of Friction
- friction is random in nature, so larger time horizons should account for more friction
- friction is compounding, two setbacks are more than twice as bad as one setback
- addressing friction can create new sources of friction, e.g. automation can lead to new bugs or devs forgetting how to do things manually

## Addressing friction
- use smaller scopes ans shorter iterations: less time for compounding friction
- give devs more autonomy: 
	- friction is difference between the model and the world
	- planning happens on a model level by necessity
	- so executing devs should have autonomy to react to friction
- redundancy: critical components and (people) functions should be redundant
- better planning: 
	- won't ever remove friction, but can help spot places of high friction in advance
	- planning process should contain writing out detailed specifications that contain a section on friction, see e.g. [[Shape Up Planning Process]]
- automation: 
	- automating processes reduces the surface for human mistakes
	- but: automation is its own source of friction
